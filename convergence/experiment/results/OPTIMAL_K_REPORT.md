# Cluster-k* Test — Method Calibration, Not a Negative Result

**STATUS: NON-PRE-REGISTERED EXPLORATORY METHOD CALIBRATION.**

Generated by `src/13_optimal_cluster_k.py`. Operates on all
four probed models' concept-probe activations.

## What we tested

Section 2.6 sub-divides projection class P_3 into P_3-rich
(k̄ ≈ 4–10), P_3-chunked (k̄ ≈ 10–30), and P_3-recognition
(k̄ ≈ 100–500) by per-coordinatee state dimensionality. The
hypothesis: within transformer middle layers, the dominant
representational structure is *richer* than at the perimeter,
so the *optimal* number of distinguishable clusters k* should
be *higher* in the middle than at the perimeter — a
within-model P_3-chunked signature.

The operationalization: k-means at k ∈ {6, 12, 20, 50};
silhouette score per k; report the k that maximizes silhouette
as k*. Cross-architecture: compute the modal k* in early /
middle / late thirds.

## What the test produced

| Model | Early k* (mode) | Middle k* (mode) | Late k* (mode) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Llama-3.2-3B | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| Mistral-7B-v0.3 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| Qwen2.5-7B | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| Llama-3.1-8B | 6 | 6 | 6 |

Modal k* = 6 across all layer-thirds in 4/4 models. The narrow
fact that survives: **language is a strong organizing axis at
small k throughout the network**, consistent with the
language-probe saturation finding.

## Why this isn't a concrete negative result

The operationalization bakes in five implicit assumptions, all
of which are known to be poor matches for transformer middle-
layer geometry. Any one of them could be the actual reason
the test didn't surface the hypothesized signature.

### Implicit assumption 1: clusters are spherical, well-separated, equal-variance

k-means is a maximum-likelihood estimator under a model where
each cluster is a spherical Gaussian with shared variance.
Transformer middle layers — with PR ≈ 140 in the same
activations — span ~140 dimensions roughly equally. That's a
high-dimensional continuous manifold, not a small number of
compact balls. k-means at any k will mis-fit this geometry,
and the model's likelihood penalty is paying for it.

### Implicit assumption 2: silhouette captures cluster quality in high-PR regimes

Silhouette score rewards small intra-cluster distance relative
to nearest-cluster distance. In a high-PR roughly-isotropic
space every point has many neighbors at similar distance, so
silhouette is near zero for *any* clustering — bad or good.
Empirically this is what we observed: middle-layer silhouette
values were 0.001–0.020 across all tested k. The metric was
*unable to distinguish good clusterings from bad ones in this
regime*. The test had no power.

### Implicit assumption 3: concept structure lives in raw activation space

The current line of interpretability work (sparse-autoencoder
monosemantic-features research) suggests concept structure
lives in *sparse* directions in transformer hidden states,
not in the raw residual-stream geometry. Decomposing
activations into a learned overcomplete sparse basis surfaces
many more interpretable directions than PCA / k-means on the
raw vectors. Looking for P_3-chunked structure via vanilla
k-means is searching for an FM signal with an AM receiver.

### Implicit assumption 4: Euclidean distance is the right metric

Transformer activations have known geometric peculiarities —
typically not isotropic in their native metric, and concept
structure is widely captured better by cosine similarity (Ng's
choice in the Sapir-Whorf post) than by Euclidean distance.
k-means is fundamentally Euclidean.

### Implicit assumption 5: cluster cardinality {6, 12, 20, 50} bounds the structure

The {6, 12, 20, 50} grid was anchored to substrate-specific
labels (6 languages, 3 XNLI classes). If the actual concept
granularity in the middle is k = 200 or k = 2000, the test
couldn't have surfaced it. The choice was the wrong anchor.

### Implicit assumption 6: snapshots, not trajectories

Concept structure may be defined by *trajectories* through
layers, not by snapshots at any single layer. A concept might
be "the direction the activation moves over layers 8–15"
rather than "where the activation sits at layer 12." Static
per-layer k-means can't capture this.

## What the test does establish

The narrow finding that survives: language is a strong axis
under small-k k-means throughout the network. This is
*consistent* with the broader experimental story:

- Language probe saturated at ≥0.99 from layer 1 (Section 4.5
  pre-registered): language is everywhere linearly recoverable.
- Cluster-NMI inverted-U (`CLUSTER_PURITY_REPORT.md`): language
  is the *dominant* clustering axis at the perimeter and not
  in the middle.
- Participation ratio inverted-U
  (`MIDDLE_LAYER_GEOMETRY_REPORT.md`): the middle uses many
  more dimensions than the perimeter.

The cluster-NMI finding shows language *does* lose dominance
in the middle (against language labels at k=6). The cluster-k*
test agrees: even where language loses dominance against
language labels, the global silhouette structure under raw
k-means is still dominated by language. These two findings
look in tension but are explained by the same fact: language
remains a high-variance axis everywhere; the middle adds many
*additional* axes that don't form k-means-shaped clusters in
raw space.

## What would actually test the hypothesis

The within-transformer P_3-chunked / P_3-recognition signature
remains an open testable question. Concrete candidate methods,
in roughly increasing order of difficulty:

### (M1) Sparse-autoencoder feature counts per layer
*Difficulty: medium (existing tooling).*
Train SAEs on per-layer activations; count the number of
distinct sparse features that activate above a frequency
threshold. Predict: feature count peaks in the middle, with a
3–10× ratio between middle and late layers — the within-model
P_3-chunked signature.

### (M2) Effective rank at multiple cosine thresholds — RUN, 4/4 POSITIVE
*Difficulty: low (no training).* Status: **completed**, see
`EFFECTIVE_RANK_M2_REPORT.md`. For each layer, compute how
many PCA components account for {50, 75, 90, 95, 99}% of
variance under raw and L2-normalized (cosine) geometry. The
inverted-U replicates **4/4 at every threshold under both
metrics**. Mean middle/late ratio at 90% variance: 2.0×
(per-model 5.7× to 65×). Cosine and raw within 5%, so the
inversion is about direction count, not magnitude. Confirms
the within-transformer P_3-chunked hypothesis under an
operationalization specifically chosen to handle high-PR
continuous-manifold geometry where M0 (k-means + silhouette)
had no power.

### (M3) Topic models with continuous (soft) loadings
*Difficulty: medium.*
LDA-class, PLSA, or NMF on activations. These find overlapping
soft clusters appropriate for high-D continuous structure
where vanilla k-means fails. Predict: optimal topic count is
higher in the middle than at the perimeter.

### (M4) Density-aware clustering (DBSCAN, HDBSCAN, mean-shift)
*Difficulty: low–medium (off-the-shelf).*
Methods that don't assume cluster shape or count. Run with
varying density parameters across layers; report cluster count
that survives at each density level.

### (M5) Trajectory-based concept extraction
*Difficulty: high.*
Define a concept as a per-token activation trajectory through
layers; cluster trajectories rather than snapshots. This
captures concept-formation dynamics that snapshot methods miss.

### (M6) Ng-style cosine similarity, but quantitative
*Difficulty: low.*
Replicate Ng's same-language-same-content vs different-
language-same-content vs different-language-different-content
cosine comparisons quantitatively across all four models, with
formal statistical tests. Direct quantitative version of his
qualitative finding.

## What this report should be cited for

When this method-calibration result is referenced elsewhere,
it should be cited as:

> *A k-means + silhouette test at k ∈ {6, 12, 20, 50} found
> language as the dominant axis at all depths, but the test
> bakes in cluster-shape and metric assumptions known to fail
> in the high-PR continuous-manifold regime that
> participation-ratio analysis identifies as the middle-layer
> geometry. M2 (effective rank at variance thresholds)
> subsequently confirmed the within-transformer P_3-chunked
> hypothesis 4/4 across architectures with mean middle/late
> ratio 2.0× at 90% variance, demonstrating that the cluster-k*
> test's null was a methodology artifact rather than a finding
> against the hypothesis.*

Not as a negative result against the framework's
sub-classification, which stands on Dunbar's empirical
5/15/50/150/500 nested-rings anchor regardless of within-
transformer operationalization.

## Why this report exists in this form

Methodological discipline: *report what was tested, what was
operationalized, what was found, and what assumptions the
operationalization made.* The earlier framing as "negative
result" understated the conditional nature of the finding. A
test that bakes in known-failing assumptions is not a test
of the underlying hypothesis; it's a test of the method.
This rewrite distinguishes the two.

The Section 2.6 sub-classification claim is unchanged. What
changes is the path forward: M1–M6 above are the next
empirical steps for surfacing the within-transformer signature,
none of which has been run.
